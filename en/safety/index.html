Safety

Control at every stage of the operation

Fire prevention, inventory control, traceability and physical security at a terminal that runs around the clock.

Approach

Four layers of control

A fuel terminal that operates 24/7 needs controls that work without interruption. At PSD they are organized in four layers: fire protection, inventory control, product traceability and physical security of the site.

This page describes those systems in general terms. For security reasons, PSD does not disclose the location of its equipment or the configuration of its protection and surveillance systems.

Systems

How the operation is protected

  • Fire prevention and protection

    The terminal is equipped with foam fire-suppression systems. Foam forms a blanket over the fuel surface that cuts off oxygen, suppresses vapors and cools the area: it is the standard extinguishing method for liquid hydrocarbon fires.

  • Inventory control

    Every tank has radar inventory gauging and automatic reconciliation. The system checks measured levels against receipts and dispatches and flags any discrepancy.

  • Traceability

    Traceability systems record how product moves through the terminal, from reception to truck dispatch, bunkering or export, so the terminal can answer a customer, an auditor or a regulator with data.

  • Physical security

    Access to the site is controlled for both people and vehicles, and the terminal operates a closed-circuit television (CCTV) surveillance system.

Inventory

Measured inventory, verifiable information

For the owner of stored product, or for the bank that finances it, knowing how much is in each tank matters as much as the terminal's capacity.

At PSD, radar gauging and automatic reconciliation in every tank give operations, customers and financial counterparties the same source of information. The same measurement underpins the reconciliation of product received, stored and dispatched.
