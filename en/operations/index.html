Operations

From river to tank, from tank to market

Reception, storage, dispatch, bunkering and export at a single terminal, with 24/7 operations and fully automated loading.

How the terminal operates

Five operations on one site

PSD combines five operations on a single site: it receives fuels from the river, stores them in radar-gauged tanks, dispatches them by truck, supplies fuel to vessels and exports ethanol and biodiesel.

The terminal runs around the clock, every day of the year. Because all five operations share the same berth, tanks and control systems, product moves from one stage to the next without leaving the terminal, and inventory is measured where it is received and where it is dispatched.

The sequence

How product moves through the terminal

Product comes in at the berth, is stored in tanks and leaves through three outlets: the truck loading rack, bunkering to vessels and the export lines.

  1. Reception

    Barges discharge at the berth on the Paraguay River, which takes two 67 m barges or vessels up to 120 m, with a 13 ft draft. Two discharge lines carry diesel and gasoline into the tanks.

    2 lines

  2. Storage

    The tank farm has 87,000 m³ of installed capacity. Inventory in every tank is gauged by radar and reconciled automatically.

    87,000 m³

  3. Dispatch

    Truck loading is fully automated across 8 loading islands and 16 bays, with a dispatch capacity of 8,000 m³ per day.

    8,000 m³/day

  4. Bunkering

    From the berth, the terminal supplies fuel to vessels at a pumping rate of 80 m³/h.

    80 m³/h

  5. Export

    Two export lines load ethanol and biodiesel onto vessels at the berth, at a pumping rate of 350 m³/h.

    350 m³/h

Dispatch

Fully automated loading

The terminal's loading rack has 8 islands and 16 bays, and truck loading is fully automated. Automation reduces manual tasks in every load and supports a dispatch capacity of 8,000 m³ per day in a 24-hour operation.

Automated loading is integrated with inventory control: automatic reconciliation in each tank checks volumes dispatched against radar gauging.

Products and lines

What moves through the terminal

  • Diesel and gasoline

    Received through two discharge lines running from the berth to the tanks.

  • Ethanol and biodiesel

    Exported through two dedicated lines, at a pumping rate of 350 m³/h.

  • Marine fuel

    Bunkering is supplied from the berth at a pumping rate of 80 m³/h.

  • Truck dispatch

    8,000 m³ per day across 8 islands and 16 bays, with fully automated loading.

Berth

One berth for river inflows and outflows

Reception, bunkering and export all run through the same berth on the Paraguay River, with a 13 ft draft.
