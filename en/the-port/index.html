The port

Grupo Martín Martín's fuel terminal on the Paraguay River

Puerto Santo Domingo receives, stores and dispatches fuels in Villeta, 33 km from Asunción, on the Paraguay–Paraná waterway.

Who we are

A proprietary terminal, fully operational

Puerto Santo Domingo (PSD) is the proprietary port terminal of Grupo Martín Martín. It covers 100,000 m² in the Villeta industrial park, has 300 m of frontage on the Paraguay River and operates around the clock, every day of the year.

On a single site, the terminal receives fuels from the river, stores them in tanks with radar inventory gauging and dispatches them by truck through fully automated loading. From its berth, it supplies fuel to vessels and exports ethanol and biodiesel.

Installed storage capacity stands at 87,000 m³, and the terminal plans to expand it to 158,800 m³.

Role in the Group

Grupo Martín Martín's port infrastructure

PSD is Grupo Martín Martín's port asset: the point where fuel arriving on the waterway is received, measured, stored and sent on to market.

Owning its terminal gives the Group direct control over three variables in its supply chain:

  • Reception capacity, with a berth on the Paraguay River and two discharge lines into tanks.
  • Available inventory, gauged by radar and automatically reconciled in every tank.
  • Dispatch speed, of up to 8,000 m³ per day across 8 loading islands and 16 bays.

In addition to the Group's own operations, the terminal provides reception, storage and dispatch services to third parties.

Location

On the Paraguay–Paraná waterway

The terminal is located in Villeta, Central Department, 33 km from Asunción, on the Paraguay River. The river is part of the Paraguay–Paraná waterway, the navigable system that links Paraguay with Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil and Uruguay and flows downstream into the Río de la Plata.

For a landlocked country, the waterway is the natural route for foreign trade. PSD sits on it with 300 m of its own frontage and a berth for two 67 m barges or vessels up to 120 m, with a 13 ft draft.

Regional connectivity. By river, the terminal connects with ports across the basin and, through the Río de la Plata, with ocean shipping. By road, it dispatches by truck from its own loading rack, 33 km from the capital.

Why PSD matters to an investor

What sets the terminal apart as an infrastructure asset.

  • An operating asset

    The terminal already receives, stores and dispatches fuels. Its expansion builds on a running operation, not on a greenfield project.
